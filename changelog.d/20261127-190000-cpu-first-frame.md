**The first frame drawn on the CPU**: a program opened by `host.host_open` on Linux shows its first picture while the graphics device, its surface and its pipelines are still being made, drawn on the CPU with the shaders' own formulas and given to the window through `wl_shm` on Wayland or `PutImage` on X11; the next frame is the GPU's, in the same place, and the two pictures differ by a few pixels at most. On lavapipe (hello, Xvfb) the first content shows about four times sooner (a median of 88 ms after the process started instead of 392, on a loaded machine). A tiling compositor's tile is drawn on the CPU too, so it shows the window at once. `render.init_window(..., cpu_first = true)` asks for it in a program with its own loop; `render.cpu_stage_active`, `render.cpu_stage_end` and `render.cpu_frame_exact` say where it is; `platform.show_pixels`, `platform.can_show_pixels` and `platform.drop_pixels` show a picture drawn on the CPU on a window. Layers, filters, blend modes, planes, pictures and application shaders are not drawn on the CPU: they show from the GPU's frame. A machine with no graphics driver gets the same sentence as before, at the same place. `TOOLKIT_CPU_FIRST=0` turns it off; `-define:TOOLKIT_CPU_FIRST=false` leaves it out of the program (64 KB of code). `TOOLKIT_CPU_SHADOW=1` draws each GPU frame on the CPU too, for a test (`render.cpu_shadow_pixels`).
